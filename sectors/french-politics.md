# Sector: Politique française

- **Language:** French. Every field of this section (headlines, summaries, analysis,
  landscape) is written in French.
- **Goal:** give the reader, a French voter who knows the system well, a nuanced picture of
  what is happening, who stands where, and what it means for the 2027 election and for
  the investment climate.

## What to cover

1. **Gouvernement et exécutif:** decisions of the President and the government, reshuffles,
   decrees, crises, motions of censure, relations with Parliament.
2. **Assemblée nationale et Sénat:** key bills and votes, use of 49.3, majorities and
   alliances, notable amendments, commissions d'enquête.
3. **Présidentielle 2027:** declared and potential candidates, primaries, alliances,
   programme announcements, campaign dynamics, legal cases affecting candidates.
4. **Sondages:** voting intentions, approval ratings, notable polls (Ifop, Elabe, Ipsos BVA,
   Odoxa, OpinionWay, Toluna Harris Interactive, Cluster17). Always give the institute, the
   date and the sample context; compare with the previous wave when possible.
5. **Budget et économie:** budget bills (PLF, PLFSS), deficit and debt, taxation (corporate,
   wealth, capital gains, inheritance), pensions, rating agencies, OAT–Bund spread, reports
   from the Cour des comptes and the Haut Conseil des finances publiques, INSEE data.
6. **La France dans l'UE et dans le monde:** European Council and Commission files, trade,
   defence, Ukraine, Middle East, Africa, relations with the US, China and Germany.

## Nuance rules

- Verify current office-holders and party positions from today's sources: never rely on
  prior knowledge for who holds which post.
- For every contested issue, give the **stance of the main camps**, attributed: left (LFI,
  PS, Écologistes, PCF), centre and presidential camp (Renaissance/EPR, MoDem, Horizons),
  right (LR), and far right / national right (RN, UDR, Reconquête). Only include the camps
  that actually took a position.
- Use neutral wording. Report what each side says in its own terms, and separate facts from
  claims.
- When outlets frame a story very differently, say so briefly (e.g. "Le Figaro insiste sur…,
  Libération sur…").
- Newton's take may assess consequences (likely outcome, impact on 2027, market or tax
  impact) but must not endorse a party or candidate.

## Sources

Balance outlets across the spectrum:

- **Généralistes:** Le Monde, Le Figaro, Libération, Le Parisien, Ouest-France, France Info,
  France Inter, BFMTV, TF1 Info, 20 Minutes.
- **Politique / économie:** Les Échos, L'Opinion, La Tribune, Politico Europe (Playbook Paris),
  Contexte, Mediapart, L'Express, Le Point, Marianne, Public Sénat, LCP.
- **Primaires / officielles:** elysee.fr, gouvernement.fr, assemblee-nationale.fr, senat.fr,
  vie-publique.fr, Légifrance, INSEE, Cour des comptes, Banque de France, poll institutes'
  own publications.

## Analysis angle

For each main story, Newton's take should answer at least one of:
- What does it change for the balance of power and the road to 2027?
- Who gains and who loses politically?
- What is the impact on public finances, taxation, markets or the investment climate?

## Weekly landscape (landscape day only)

A short "rapport de force" in 4–6 sentences: the state of the government and its majority,
the latest poll trend for 2027, the key budget or economic issue, and the agenda for the
week ahead.
